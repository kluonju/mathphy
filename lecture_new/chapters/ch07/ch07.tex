Earlier chapters solved definite problems mainly by separation of variables. The \textbf{Green's function method} takes a complementary route. A Green's function---also called a \textbf{point-source influence function}---is the field produced by a unit point source subject to prescribed boundary and/or initial conditions. Once that response is known, linearity yields the field of an arbitrary source by superposition.

This chapter follows the classical development for Poisson's equation, the method of images, time-dependent wave and heat problems, the impulse theorem, and (optionally) generalized Green formulas for elliptic, parabolic, and hyperbolic operators.
